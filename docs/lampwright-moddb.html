Lampwright

A belt that lights your way while you wear it — so both hands and the offhand slot stay free for the axe, the bow, or the shield you actually came down here with.

Vintage Story only lights you from what you are holding. Carry a torch and you give up a hand; carry a lantern and you give up the offhand. Pick up a two-handed weapon, a bow or a shield and you walk blind.

Lampwright's Belt is a tinker's belt with four temporal gears set into the leather, feeding a small brass lamp at the hip. Wear it and it lights the ground around you from the waist slot — which costs you nothing you were using — at light level 17, brighter than a torch and a shade under a lantern. It never burns down and never needs filling.

It was built for playing with heavy lighting or path-tracing shaders, where the world is genuinely dark and a free hand matters. It is just as useful in a vanilla cave.

Before you install
Dynamic lights have to be switched on. If your client has Max Dynamic Lights set to 0, you will see no glow at all — from this belt or from a held torch. That is a client graphics setting no mod can override; the game's default is 10, and anything above 0 is enough.

Crafting

A ring in the 3x3 grid, around any waist belt you already own:

gear twine gear
twine belt twine
gear twine gear
  • 4x temporal gear, in the corners
  • 4x flax twine, on the edges
  • 1x any waist belt, in the centre

The gears go in four separate cells because a temporal gear stacks to one, so a recipe asking for a stack of four could never be satisfied. Any vanilla belt works as the base — the plain leather one is the cheapest.

That gives you the temporal belt, which glows cyan. The other five colours come out of a barrel.

Six colours

Seal the belt in a barrel with dye for 4 hours, the same way you dye cloth and daub. Pour the dye in by holding the bucket and right-clicking the barrel — liquid does not go in through the barrel window — then open the barrel and drop the belt into its item slot. Any amount from 1 litre up works, and only 1 litre is spent, so a single bucket does the whole set:

  • Temporal — cyan, the colour it is crafted at. No dye; a temporal gear puts it back
  • Orange — the exact colour of a lit torch. Orange dye, from yellow dye and an onion
  • Red — red dye, from crushed cinnabar
  • Yellow — warm, close to a gas lamp. Yellow dye, from an onion or a heather flower
  • Blue — a cool sky blue. Blue dye, from a cornflower or crushed lapis lazuli
  • White — neutral. White dye, from borax powder — the only one that is not grown or mined near the surface

The dyes themselves are barrel work too: water and the ingredient, sealed 8 hours. Yellow and blue are the cheap ones, an onion or a cornflower away. Orange is the only two-stage dye, made from yellow rather than from water. White is the long errand — borax only forms in sedimentary rock, so it means claystone, sandstone, shale, chalk, limestone, chert or conglomerate, or an Alba trader.

Any colour re-dyes straight to any other, so you never have to pass back through temporal. Putting a temporal gear in the barrel in place of the dye fits the original lens back and returns it to cyan.

The light the belt casts always matches the lens you can see on the model, and the glow lands on you as well as on the ground around you.

Wear and repair

The belt is ordinary clothing. It wears down as you use it, gives 0.3°C of warmth, and is mended the way any garment is — pick the material up on your cursor and click it onto the belt:

  • Sewing kit — +75% condition
  • Cloth or linen — +50%
  • Flax twine — +10%

A trip through the dye barrel also mends it. Barrel crafting hands back a fresh item rather than carrying the old one across, so a belt comes out of a re-dye at full condition whatever state it went in at.

Condition only scales the warmth. A belt worn down to its last threads still lights at full strength.

How the light works

The belt is a moving point light, the same kind the game already gives a held torch. Nothing is baked into chunk lighting, so there is no relight cost as you walk and no bright patches left behind you.

It does not replace what is in your hands. A torch and the belt stack rather than cancelling each other, so you can throw light down a tunnel ahead while the belt covers your feet.

Other players see it too, provided the server has the mod. The light is published by the server and drawn by every client in range, so a lit belt reads across a multiplayer world rather than only to the person wearing it.

Compatibility

Needed on the server as well
This is not a client-side mod. It adds an item, and the server is what tells every client that a belt is lit — so installing it only on your client will not work when you join a multiplayer server. It has to be on the server too. In singleplayer, installing it covers both.

No dependencies. Built and tested against Vintage Story 1.22.7. The belt is an ordinary item in an ordinary clothing slot, so adding or removing the mod disturbs nothing in a world beyond the belts themselves.
